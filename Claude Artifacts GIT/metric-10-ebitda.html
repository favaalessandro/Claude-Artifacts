💰 Metrica 10: EBITDA & Net Profit Margin

Buffett-Munger Skepticism Test - Capital Intensity & Real Profitability

Come le margini reali rivelano la vera qualità economica del business e le insidie delle metriche non-GAAP

Tier 1 - Business Quality (40% weight)

📌 Perché le Margini Reali Importano (Buffett-Munger Principle)

Charlie Munger: "Ogni volta che sento EBITDA, voglio sostituire 'bullshit earnings'. È una metrica pericolosa perché ignora completamente la realtà che la depreciation rappresenta capitale REALE che deve essere reinvestito nel business."
Warren Buffett: "EBITDA è utile solo per una cosa: confrontare aziende con diverse strutture di capitale o situazioni fiscali. Come metrica di valutazione è una trappola mortale. Sempre confronta EBITDA a Operating Margin e CFO."
🚨 EBITDA Non è Profitto Reale
Cosa ignora EBITDA:
❌ Depreciation & Amortization (sono costi REALI che devono essere reinvestiti)
❌ Interest Expense (debito deve essere pagato)
❌ Taxes (azienda paga tasse sul reddito reale)
❌ Working Capital Changes (cash tied up in operations)

Problema:
EBITDA $500M sembra fantastico finché non realizzi che la depreciation è $200M/anno (capitale da reinvestire). Il vero profitto disponibile agli azionisti è molto minore.

Esempi:
Compagnia Aerea: EBITDA 25% ma Operating Margin 5% (perché depreciation è 20% di revenue)
Software: EBITDA 30% e Operating Margin 28% (solo 2% depreciation)
🎯 Net Profit Margin è la Vera Realtà
Net Profit Margin = Verità Ultima

Formula: NI ÷ Revenue × 100

Ciò che rimane DOPO:
✅ Depreciation & Amortization
✅ Interest Expense
✅ Taxes
✅ Tutti i costi operativi

Questa è l'unica metrica che importa per gli azionisti.

Una compagnia con:
• 30% Gross Margin
• 15% Operating Margin
• 5% Net Profit Margin

Quella è la vera qualità economica. Tutto il resto tra EBITDA e Net Income è "lavaggio di denaro" contabile.
⚠️ Il Pericolo della Manipolazione
Perché le Aziende Promuovono EBITDA:

1. EBITDA spesso sembra MIGLIORE che Net Income
2. Possiede ignorare la realtà del reinvestimento
3. I CFO sanno che gli analisti lo usano

Quando Diffidare:
• Azienda parla di EBITDA ma tace su Net Income
• EBITDA margin ampia ma Operating Margin ridotta
• "Adjusted EBITDA" significativamente > EBITDA riportato

Buffett's Rule:
"Se un CEO enfatizza EBITDA nel quarterly call, sono cauto. EBITDA è il primo segnale che i veri numeri non sono così belli."
💡 La Giusta Interpretazione delle Margini
Quando EBITDA è Utile:
✅ Comparare aziende con diverse strutture di capitale
✅ Industrie con diverse politiche di depreciation
✅ Aziende acquisite (goodwill può distorcere)

Quando EBITDA è Ingannevole:
❌ Come principale metrica di valutazione
❌ Quando comparato a FCF (non è equivalente)
❌ Per capital-intensive businesses (ignora reality)

Ordine di Importanza:
1. Net Profit Margin (THE TRUTH)
2. Operating Margin (operational efficiency)
3. Gross Margin (pricing power)
4. EBITDA Margin (useful for comparisons ONLY)
5. Adjusted EBITDA (use with extreme caution)

🔑 5 Dimensioni Critiche del Margin Quality

1️⃣ EBITDA vs. Operating Margin Gap
Cosa Misura: (EBITDA Margin - Operating Margin) = D&A come % di Revenue

Piccolo Gap <3pp = Asset-light (good)
Medium Gap 3-8pp = Normal manufacturing
Large Gap >8pp = Capital-intensive (watch)

Significato:
• Quanto del "profitto" EBITDA è realmente reinvestimento?
• Gap ampio = molto capitale richiesto per mantenere business
2️⃣ Operating Margin Trend
Preferito:
• Stabile o espandente (pricing power)
• >15% per software/tech
• >10% per manufacturing

Red Flag:
• Declino 2+ anni (competitive pressure)
• Volatile (cyclical/unstable)
• Divergenza EBITDA ma stabile = D&A crescente
3️⃣ Net Profit Margin (The Truth)
Questo è quello che conta.

Dopo TUTTI i costi, tasse, depreciation:
• Quanto rimane per gli azionisti?
• Sostenibile nel tempo?
• Comparabile ai peer?

>10%: Excellent
5-10%: Good
<5%: Watch
4️⃣ EBITDA to FCF Conversion
La Riconciliazione Vera:

EBITDA $500M
- Working Capital ∆
= Operating Cash Flow
- CapEx
= Free Cash Flow

Se FCF << EBITDA = Red Flag
Se FCF ≈ EBITDA = Quality (capital-light)
5️⃣ Adjusted EBITDA Risk
Attento agli Adjustments:

Piccoli (<5%) = Normale, accettabile
Moderati (5-15%) = Monitor
Grandi (>15%) = Red flag

Domanda:
Questi items davvero non ricorrono? O il management sta nascondendo?

📐 Definizioni Formali & Formule

EBITDA: Definizione
EBITDA = Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation & Amortization

Formula Classica:
EBITDA = Net Income + Interest + Taxes + Depreciation + Amortization

Formula Alternativa (da Operating Income):
EBITDA = Operating Income + Depreciation + Amortization

Significato Italiano:
EBITDA è il reddito PRIMA di:
• Interessi (costo del debito)
• Tasse (dovute al governo)
• Depreciation & Amortization (perdita di valore asset)

Semplice Esempio:
Revenue $1,000
- COGS $500
- OpEx $200
= Operating Income $300
+ Depreciation $100 (non-cash)
= EBITDA $400
📊 EBITDA Margin
Formula:
EBITDA Margin = (EBITDA ÷ Revenue) × 100

Cosa Misura:
Quale % di ogni euro di revenue rimane come profitto PRIMA di depreciation, interesse, tasse

Benchmark:
• Software: 40-50%
• Retail: 10-15%
• Manufacturing: 15-25%
• Utilities: 35-45%
• Airline: 20-25%

IMPORTANTE:
Parla solo di OPERAZIONI, non di come l'azienda è finanziata
💰 Operating Margin
Formula:
Operating Margin = (Operating Income ÷ Revenue) × 100

Equivalente a:
EBITDA Margin - (D&A as % of Revenue)

Cosa Misura:
Profitto operativo reale DOPO depreciation

Perché è Importante:
Questa è la vera misura di operational efficiency.
Se Operating Margin basso, il business non è profittevole operativamente (indipendentemente da EBITDA)
📈 Net Profit Margin
Formula:
Net Profit Margin = (Net Income ÷ Revenue) × 100

Cosa Misura:
Profitto finale disponibile agli azionisti
DOPO ogni costo, interesse, tassa, depreciation

Buffett Benchmark:
Questa è L'UNICA metrica che davvero importa per l'investitore

Uso Pratico:
• Confronto peer: Quale azienda ha il vero profitto?
• Valutazione: DCF basato su Net Income (non EBITDA)
⚠️ Adjusted EBITDA
Formula:
Adjusted EBITDA = EBITDA ± Management Adjustments

Tipici Adjustments:
+ Stock-based compensation
+ One-time charges
+ Restructuring costs
+ Litigation settlements

ATTENZIONE:
Questi "one-time" items spesso RICORRONO
Managment crea "adjusted" per farsi sembrare meglio

Regola Buffett:
Ignora Adjusted EBITDA a favore di reported

⚠️ 8 Pericoli Critici dell'EBITDA

🚨 PERICOLO #1: EBITDA Ignora Completamente la Realtà del Reinvestimento
EBITDA $500M sembra fantastico. Ma se l'azienda ha $200M di depreciation annuale, significa che per mantenere il business al livello attuale, deve reinvestire $200M ogni anno. Il vero profitto (dopo reinvestimento necessario) è meno della metà di quello che EBITDA suggerirebbe.
Scenario: Compagnia Aerea Revenue: $10B EBITDA: $2.5B (25% margin - sembra ottimo!) Depreciation: $2B (aerei, infrastruttura) Operating Income: $500M D&A: $2B (reinvestimento necessario) Domanda: Se elimino reinvestimento per avere $2.5B in cash, che succede? Risposta: In 3 anni gli aerei sono deteriorati, la compagnia crolla EBITDA è IRRELEVANTE; ciò che importa è se Operating Income copre i costi
🚨 PERICOLO #2: EBITDA Vs. Operating Cash Flow Mismatch
Se EBITDA cresce ma Operating Cash Flow cala (o rimane flat), il business ha problemi strutturali nel convertire earnings a cash. EBITDA ignora working capital changes che assorbono cash.
Segno di Deterioramento: Year 1: EBITDA +15% YoY, OCF +2% YoY (divergenza!) Year 2: EBITDA +18% YoY, OCF flat Year 3: EBITDA still growing, OCF negative Interpretazione: Azienda sta accumulando inventory/receivables Il "profitto" EBITDA è illusione; cash flow mostra verità Red Flag = STOP Investing
🚨 PERICOLO #3: Adjusted EBITDA come Gioco di Prestigio
Le aziende aggiungono "non-recurring" items per fare Adjusted EBITDA sembrare meglio di EBITDA reported. Ma questi items spesso ricorrono ogni anno (la definizione stessa di recurring!).
Rapporto Earnings: CEO: "Reported EBITDA $100M, ma adjusted EBITDA è $150M" Adjustments: Stock-based comp $30M + Restructuring $20M Red Flag #1: Perché stock-based compensation non è un costo reale? È vero costo; dilisce gli azionisti esattamente come cash payment Red Flag #2: "Restructuring" riappare OGNI ANNO 2019: "One-time restructuring" 2020: "One-time restructuring" 2021: "One-time restructuring" Verdetto: Adjusted EBITDA $150M è finzione; reported $100M è la realtà
🚨 PERICOLO #4: EBITDA Ignora Completamente la Qualità del Business Model
Due aziende con identico EBITDA margin ma diversa Operating Margin hanno radicalmente diversi qualità. Quella con Operating Margin alto ha moat; quella con Operating Margin basso no.
Azienda A (Software): EBITDA Margin: 35% Operating Margin: 32% D&A: 3% of revenue Azienda B (Airline): EBITDA Margin: 25% Operating Margin: 5% D&A: 20% of revenue Identico EBITDA margin = diversi business model Azienda A ha moat (pricing power, asset-light) Azienda B è commodity (capital-intensive, competitive) EBITDA nasconderebbe la differenza; Operating Margin la rivela
🚨 PERICOLO #5: EBITDA Usato per "Giustificare" M&A che Distrugge Valore
Le aziende promettono "EBITDA synergies" da acquisizioni per giustificare multipli alti. Questi synergies spesso non materializzano, l'acquisizione distrugge valore, e EBITDA è stato il strumento di manipolazione.
Esempio Reale: AT&T acquista T-Mobile per $39B "Pro Forma EBITDA Synergies": $6B + $3B in cost cuts Realtà: - Synergies materializzati: $2B (mancamento $7B promesso) - Acquisizione distrusse $15B+ in shareholder value - EBITDA projections erano bugie usate per convincere board Lezione: EBITDA-based M&A valuations spesso falliscono Focus su FCF impact, non EBITDA "synergies"
🚨 PERICOLO #6: EBITDA Ignora il Costo del Debito
Due aziende identiche per operazione ma con diverso leverage hanno identico EBITDA ma molto diversi profitti netti. EBITDA non capisce il debito; Net Income sì.
Azienda A (Leveraged): EBITDA: $100M Interest: $30M (high debt) Net Income: $50M Azienda B (Conservative): EBITDA: $100M Interest: $5M (low debt) Net Income: $75M Identico EBITDA, ma Azienda B è molto più profittevole e safe EBITDA nasconde il rischio; Net Income lo mostra
🚨 PERICOLO #7: EBITDA Usato per Mascherare Deterioramento Operativo
Azienda taglia R&D, reduce headcount, defers maintenance CapEx per boost EBITDA short-term. Operazioni degradano, ma EBITDA sembra bello finché non collassa improvvisamente.
Scenario: Software Company che Degradava 2015: EBITDA Margin 30%, R&D 18% of revenue 2016: EBITDA Margin 32% (CEO says "efficiency gains") Actually: R&D cut to 14% (future competitiveness damaged) 2017: EBITDA Margin 34% (looks great!) But: Customers switching to competitors (innovation lagged) 2018: EBITDA Margin 25% (sudden collapse as competitive position eroded) EBITDA margin espandente nascose la realtà del deterioramento
🚨 PERICOLO #8: Confrontare EBITDA Tra Industrie con Diverse D&A Policies
D&A varia enormemente per depreciation policy, asset life assumptions, e goodwill. Due aziende simili in realtà possono avere EBITDA margin molto diversi se depreciation policy differisce.
Azienda A: Conservative depreciation (5-year) = Alta D&A = EBITDA margin = 30%, Operating margin = 20% Azienda B: Aggressive depreciation (10-year) = Bassa D&A = EBITDA margin = 35%, Operating margin = 28% Stesso underlying business, ma EBITDA margin differirebbe di 500bp Lezione: Non comparare EBITDA margin tra aziende con diverso D&A policy Usa Operating Margin (o FCF) per comparison

⚡ La Regola Aurea Buffett-Munger su EBITDA

"EBITDA è una metrica utile esattamente in UNA situazione: comparare aziende con strutture di capitale diversi. In ogni altra situazione, è una trappola. Quando valuto una azienda, guardo SEMPRE a: Operating Margin, Net Profit Margin, Free Cash Flow. Ignorodella EBITDA a meno che sia rilevante per il confronto specifico. Ricorda: Depreciation è REALE. Non è 'aggiunto indietro' solo perché è non-cash. Ogni dollar di depreciation rappresenta capitale che DEVE essere reinvestito. Ignorare questo è stupidità." - Charlie Munger, parafrasato

🔧 5-Step Framework: Analisi EBITDA & Net Profit Margin

Step 1️⃣: Calcolare EBITDA e EBITDA Margin (Verificata)

Cosa fare: Calcola EBITDA usando formula riportata sopra; verifica contro company-provided EBITDA se disponibile.

Formula: EBITDA = NI + Interest + Taxes + D&A

Calcola Margin: EBITDA Margin = (EBITDA / Revenue) × 100

  • Verifica: Company reports EBITDA che corrisponde ai tuoi calcoli
  • Red flag: Grandi discrepanze indicano accounting oddities
Esempio: Microsoft Fiscale 2023 Revenue: $198.3B Operating Income: $78B D&A: $8.5B EBITDA: $86.5B EBITDA Margin: 43.6% Assessment: Margin eccezionale (software company) ✅

Step 2️⃣: Confronta EBITDA Margin a Operating Margin (Capital Intensity Test)

Cosa fare: Calcola il gap tra EBITDA e Operating Margin; questo rivela D&A come % di revenue.

Formula: EBITDA Margin - Operating Margin = D&A as % of Revenue

Interpretazione:

  • Gap <3pp: Asset-light business (excellent, minimal reinvestment needed)
  • Gap 3-8pp: Normal manufacturing/capital-intensive (acceptable)
  • Gap >8pp: Very capital-intensive (watch carefully)
  • Gap >15pp: Extremely capital-intensive (significant annual reinvestment required)
Comparison: Software Company (Asset-Light): EBITDA Margin: 40% Operating Margin: 37% Gap: 3pp → Only 3% of revenue is depreciation; business is asset-light ✅ Airline (Capital-Intensive): EBITDA Margin: 25% Operating Margin: 5% Gap: 20pp → 20% of revenue is depreciation; massive reinvestment required ⚠️ Interpretation: Airline "profitability" of 25% EBITDA is illusory Real operating profitability only 5% The gap is reinvestment requirement

Step 3️⃣: Analizzare Net Profit Margin (The Ultimate Truth)

Cosa fare: Calcola Net Profit Margin (NI/Revenue); verifica trend 5-10 anni.

Formula: Net Profit Margin = (Net Income / Revenue) × 100

Assessment:

  • Questa è l'UNICA metrica che davvero importa per gli azionisti
  • Contrasta: Azienda con 40% EBITDA margin potrebbe avere 5% net margin
  • Red flag: EBITDA alto ma Net Margin basso = problema strutturale
Reality Check: Company reports: - Revenue: $1B - EBITDA: $400M (40% - sounds great!) - EBITDA Margin: 40% - But: Net Profit Margin only 3% Why the gap? - D&A: $200M - Interest: $80M - Taxes: $40M - Remaining: $80M net income Real margin: 8% (not 40%!) EBITDA misled; Net Income told the truth

Step 4️⃣: Verificare EBITDA a CFO Reconciliation

Cosa fare: Riconcilia EBITDA a Operating Cash Flow; grande divergenza = red flag.

Formula: EBITDA → Operating Cash Flow (adj. working capital) → Free Cash Flow (adj. CapEx)

Assessment:

  • Se EBITDA >> CFO: Possibile manipulation o working capital problem
  • Se EBITDA ≈ CFO: Clean earnings, quality business
  • Se EBITDA < CFO: Conservative accounting (positive)
Red Flag Example: Company reports EBITDA $500M But Operating Cash Flow only $250M (50% conversion!) Why? - Receivables increasing (customers paying later) - Inventory piling up (sales strategy or demand declining?) - Payables decreasing (paying suppliers faster?) Verdict: EBITDA is inflated; real cash generation is half The business has structural cash flow problems

Step 5️⃣: Valutare Sostenibilità & Scalabilità

Cosa fare: Determina se le margini sono sostenibili o dipendenti da fattori temporanei.

Assessment:

  • Trend expanding margins: Positive (moat widening, pricing power growing)
  • Trend stable margins: Good (sustainable business model)
  • Trend declining margins: Negative (competitive pressure or costs rising)
  • Volatile margins: Red flag (cyclical or unstable)
Buffett Assessment Question: "If this company couldn't cut R&D or defer maintenance, would margins hold?" YES → Sustainable moat ✅ NO → Temporary margins, unsustainable 🔴 Example: Company expanding EBITDA margin by cutting R&D = Unsustainable (future competitiveness damaged) Company expanding EBITDA margin by pricing power = Sustainable moat (customers willing to pay more)

📊 La Lezione Critica: EBITDA Margin vs. Intensità Capitale

🟢 Asset-Light Business (Buffett Ideal)
Caratteristiche:
• EBITDA - Operating Margin gap: <3pp
• Depreciation <5% of revenue
• Minimal reinvestment required
• Free Cash Flow ≈ Operating Income - Taxes

Esempi: Software, Insurance, Professional Services, Brand Companies

Buffett Quote: "This is the business I want to own. Generates profits that require minimal reinvestment."

Valuation: Premium multiple justified (25-30x earnings)
Why: Cash generation superior; growth doesn't require proportional capital

Example: Apple
- EBITDA Margin: 32%
- Operating Margin: 29%
- Gap: 3pp (minimal reinvestment)
- Can grow without capital constraints
🟡 Normal Capital-Intensive (Expected Return)
Charakteristiken:
• EBITDA - Operating Margin gap: 3-8pp
• Depreciation 3-8% of revenue
• Moderate reinvestment required
• Free Cash Flow = 60-80% of Operating Income

Esempi: Manufacturing, Retail, Distribution, Real Estate

Caratteristica: Business quality acceptable but not exceptional

Valuation: Fair multiple (15-20x earnings)
Why: Capital requirements limit growth; reinvestment must match growth rate

Example: Typical Retailer
- EBITDA Margin: 18%
- Operating Margin: 10%
- Gap: 8pp (healthy reinvestment)
- Growth limited by CapEx availability
🔴 Highly Capital-Intensive (Caution Required)
Karakteristiken:
• EBITDA - Operating Margin gap: 8-15pp
• Depreciation 8-15% of revenue
• Substantial reinvestment required
• Free Cash Flow = 40-60% of Operating Income

Esempi: Airlines, Railroads, Utilities, Telecommunications, Heavy Manufacturing

Problem: EBITDA margin can be highly misleading
A 25% EBITDA margin might be only 10% Operating Margin

Valuation: Discount multiple required (10-15x earnings)
Why: Capital intensity limits true profitability; growth requires substantial investment

Example: Airline Industry
- EBITDA Margin: 25% (looks decent)
- Operating Margin: 5% (reality)
- D&A Gap: 20pp (replacement of planes)
- Growth impossible without new aircraft financing
🔴🔴 Extremely Capital-Intensive (Avoid Territory)
Karakteristiken:
• EBITDA - Operating Margin gap: >15pp
• Depreciation >15% of revenue
• Massive reinvestment required
• Free Cash Flow = <40% of Operating Income

Esempi: Mining, Energy Infrastructure, Heavy Construction, Refineries

The Problem:
EBITDA is nearly fiction in these businesses
20% EBITDA margin might equal 0-3% Net Profit Margin

Buffett Approach: Generally avoid entirely
Why: Capital requirements consume all profits; growth impossible without external capital/leverage

Example: Mining Company
- EBITDA Margin: 30% (commodity pricing luck)
- D&A (Depletion): 25% of revenue
- Operating Margin: 5%
- Only 5% of revenue available for interest, taxes, growth

Verdict: Highly cyclical; margins vanish when commodity prices fall

⚠️ 10 Red Flags nel Margin Analysis

🚨 RED FLAG #1: EBITDA Growing ma Operating Margin Declining
EBITDA up 10%, Operating Margin down 2pp = company increasing depreciation (aging asset base or aggressive capitalization). Future margins under pressure as assets require replacement.
Segnale negativo: EBITDA Growth: +10% YoY Operating Margin: -200bp compression D&A as % Revenue: Expanding Interpretazione: Asset base aging; replacement capex coming Azione: Investigate depreciation policy and asset age
🚨 RED FLAG #2: EBITDA Margin Expanding but Free Cash Flow Declining
The clearest sign that margin expansion is accounting fiction. Real cash generation (FCF) falling while reported profitability (EBITDA) rising = working capital or CapEx problem.
Segnale negativo: EBITDA Margin: 30% → 32% → 34% (expanding) Free Cash Flow: $50M → $40M → $20M (collapsing!) Cause possibili: 1. Inventory building (cash tied up) 2. Receivables increasing (collection problems) 3. CapEx deferred (maintenance crisis coming) Verdetto: EBITDA is fiction; real problem is cash flow
🚨 RED FLAG #3: Adjusted EBITDA >> Reported EBITDA
Adjustments of >5% indicate management is massaging the numbers. Items claimed as "non-recurring" may actually recur annually.
Segnale negativo: Reported EBITDA: $80M Management Adjustments: +$30M (37% adjustment!) Adjusted EBITDA: $110M Red flags in adjustments: - Stock-based compensation (recurs every year) - Restructuring charges (occurred 2019, 2020, 2021) - Amortization of acquisition intangibles (recurs by definition) Verdetto: Adjusted EBITDA is fiction; use reported
🚨 RED FLAG #4: EBITDA Margin Above Peer Average by 5+ Points
Either company has genuine moat (good) or margins are unsustainable due to temporary factors (bad). Investigate why. Compare Operating Margin to peers (cleaner comparison).
Segnale negativo: Peer EBITDA Margin Average: 15% Company EBITDA Margin: 22% (7pp above peers!) Possible explanations: 1. Better operations (moat) ✅ 2. Temporary pricing power (commodity cycle) ❌ 3. Cost-cutting that damages future (R&D cuts) ❌ 4. Higher leverage (financial engineering) ❌ Investigation needed: Why are margins so much better? Can they be sustained if competition intensifies?
🚨 RED FLAG #5: Operating Margin Below Peers Despite High EBITDA Margin
Indicates higher depreciation than peers (older asset base, aggressive capitalization, or different depreciation policy). Real operational efficiency is lower than EBITDA suggests.
Segnale negativo: Company EBITDA Margin: 25% Company Operating Margin: 8% EBITDA-Operating Gap: 17pp Peer EBITDA Margin: 25% Peer Operating Margin: 18% Peer Gap: 7pp Interpretazione: Company has 10pp higher depreciation than peers Asset base much older or different accounting policy Real operational quality lower than peer (Operating Margin shows truth)
🚨 RED FLAG #6: EBITDA Margin Expanding While Revenue Growth Slowing
Margin expansion + revenue slowdown = cost-cutting likely the driver, not operational leverage. Dangerous for future competitiveness.
Segnale negativo: Year 1: Revenue +15%, EBITDA Margin 20% Year 2: Revenue +5%, EBITDA Margin 22% (200bp expansion) Year 3: Revenue -2%, EBITDA Margin 24% (200bp expansion) Pattern: Margin expanding as revenue growth disappears Cause: Cost cuts (R&D, headcount, marketing) boosting margin short-term Risk: Future revenues suffering from underinvestment Growth cannot be sacrificed for margins
🚨 RED FLAG #7: Net Profit Margin Much Lower Than Operating Margin
Gap between Operating Margin and Net Profit Margin shows impact of interest, taxes, and other items. Large gap = high leverage, high tax rate, or unusual items.
Segnale negativo: Operating Margin: 20% Net Profit Margin: 5% (15pp gap!) Why the collapse? - Interest: 7% (high debt) - Taxes: 5% (structural or one-time issue) - Other: 3% (unusual charges) Question: Is high debt sustainable if operating margin compressed? What if operating margin declines further?
🚨 RED FLAG #8: D&A Growing Faster Than Revenue
Asset depreciation accelerating faster than revenue = asset base aging, requiring maintenance capex, or acquisition goodwill/intangibles amortizing. Future CapEx spike likely.
Segnale negativo: Revenue Growth: +5% CAGR D&A Growth: +12% CAGR (much faster!) Interpretazione: D&A growing faster than revenue unsustainable Caused by: Asset purchases that haven't generated revenue yet Risk: If D&A continues at 12% while revenue stays at 5%, Operating Margin will deteriorate 7pp annually Eventually becomes unsustainable
🚨 RED FLAG #9: Significant One-Time Items Affecting Net Income
Large asset sales, litigation settlements, or accounting changes may distort Net Profit Margin. Adjust NI to remove one-time items for trend analysis.
Segnale negativo: Net Income: $100M One-time items: $40M gain (asset sale) Adjusted NI: $60M Actual Net Margin: 5% (but looks like 10% without adjustment) Verdetto: Don't use reported margin; calculate adjusted margin Remove one-time gains/charges for accurate assessment
🚨 RED FLAG #10: Different Margin Across Years Without Clear Explanation
Volatility in EBITDA or Net Profit margins (>2-3pp annual swings) indicates business is cyclical or unstable. Difficult to forecast or value confidently.
Segnale negativo: EBITDA Margin: 20% → 25% → 18% → 28% → 15% Volatility: ±5pp annual swings (unpredictable) Interpretazione: Cyclical business or operational instability Buffett would avoid: "I want boring, predictable margins" Question: What drives volatility? Can I predict next year's margin with confidence? If not = higher risk = lower valuation multiple

💼 5 Case Studies Reali - EBITDA Quality Analysis

💻 Caso 1: Apple - Exceptional Margin Quality
Profilo:
EBITDA Margin: 32% | Operating Margin: 29% | Net Profit Margin: 25%
Gap EBITDA-Operating: 3pp (asset-light) | Gap Operating-Net: 4pp (low leverage)

Analisi:
• D&A only 3% of revenue (minimal depreciation)
• Operating margin nearly equals EBITDA (what you see is what you get)
• Net margin 25% = true shareholder profitability
• No leverage issues (interest minimal)
• Adjusted EBITDA ≈ Reported (clean earnings)

Verdetto: 🟢🟢 GREEN (14/14 pts)
Textbook Buffett-quality business: High margins, asset-light, sustainable, clean earnings. EBITDA margin is trustworthy because Operating Margin confirms it. No manipulation evident.
✈️ Caso 2: Airline (United, American, Southwest)
Profilo:
EBITDA Margin: 25% | Operating Margin: 5% | Net Profit Margin: <2%
Gap EBITDA-Operating: 20pp (capital-intensive!) | Adjusted EBITDA: Often 10-15% premium

Analisi:
• 20pp gap EBITDA-Operating = 20% of revenue is depreciation
• EBITDA margin of 25% is FICTION; real margin only 5%
• Net margin <2% = business barely profitable for shareholders
• Adjusted EBITDA adds back "one-time" items that recur annually
• High leverage (interest significant) destroys profitability

The Trap:
Analyst says: "Airlines have 25% EBITDA margins"
Buffett says: "No, they have 5% operating margins and negative FCF after CapEx"

Verdetto: 🔴 RED (2/14 pts)
EBITDA is dangerous lie in airline industry. Capital requirements consume all profitability. Avoid using EBITDA for valuation.
🏪 Caso 3: Retailer with Deferred Maintenance
Profilo:
EBITDA Margin: 20% | Operating Margin: 15% | Net Profit Margin: 4%
Gap EBITDA-Operating: 5pp | But CapEx declining while store base aging

Analisi:
• Reported margins look acceptable
• But EBITDA-Operating gap growing as depreciation increases
• CapEx declining vs. historical (stores under-maintained)
• EBITDA margin artificially high due to deferred maintenance
• Future CapEx spike will crash FCF

The Problem:
Year 1: Defer maintenance → CapEx $50M, EBITDA up to 20%
Year 2: Stores deteriorate → CapEx $100M (catch-up needed)
Year 3: Customer defection begins → Revenues decline

Verdetto: 🟡→🔴 YELLOW TURNING RED (6/14 pts)
Margins unsustainable. Deferred maintenance means true capital requirement understated. Watch CapEx trend carefully; the reset is coming.
📱 Caso 4: Software Company - High Quality
Profilo:
EBITDA Margin: 38% | Operating Margin: 35% | Net Profit Margin: 28%
Gap EBITDA-Operating: 3pp | Recurring revenue: 95% of total
EBITDA-CFO conversion: 90% | Adjusted EBITDA: <2% difference

Analisi:
• Asset-light model (minimal depreciation)
• Operating margin nearly equals EBITDA (margins trustworthy)
• Recurring revenue = predictable margins
• High cash conversion (CFO ~EBITDA)
• Clean earnings (minimal adjustments)
• Sustainable without heavy reinvestment

Verdetto: 🟢🟢 GREEN (14/14 pts)
Exceptional quality. EBITDA margin is reliable indicator. Asset-light model + recurring revenue + minimal capex = Buffett ideal. Margins highly sustainable.
🏭 Caso 5: Manufacturing with Rising D&A
Profilo:
EBITDA Margin: 22% | Operating Margin: 18% (3yr ago: 22%!)
Gap EBITDA-Operating: Growing from 8pp to 12pp
D&A as % Revenue: Growing from 8% to 12%

The Danger:
Investor sees EBITDA stable at 22% and thinks business healthy
But Operating Margin declining shows structural deterioration
Future years' Operating Margin will deteriorate further

Verdetto: 🟡→🔴 YELLOW TURNING RED (6/14 pts)
EBITDA masking deterioration. D&A growth unsustainable. Operating margin is the truth-teller here; it's declining while EBITDA rises. Risk increasing.
